# Comic 016 Sketch Prompt V2: Reference Style

It makes each of the first three panel captions name the two present operating-model pillars and the one missing pillar. It also strengthens Panel 2 so the viewer can tell the engineer driving the AI use-case vehicle is the same person trying to build the capacity lane.

## Pre-Production Design Check

- Core message: AI use cases scale when capability, access, and capacity are all aligned around the right use cases.
- Causal chain: each pilot has two operating-model pillars but lacks the third; each pilot creates visible activity but cannot reach scaled outcomes; multiplying those mismatches creates enterprise traffic.
- Visual contrast: two visible present pillars in each setup versus one obvious missing pillar blocking movement.
- Panel logic: Panel 1 has capability and capacity but no access, Panel 2 has capability and access but no capacity, Panel 3 has capacity and access but no capability, and Panel 4 shows those mismatches accumulating at enterprise scale.
- Final consequence: the enterprise road network jam is earned because each prior panel contributes one different stuck vehicle pattern.
- Punchline: "Misalignment scales traffic, not outcomes" remains the main repeatable line.
- Clutter risk: the captions now carry the pillar logic, so avoid adding dense status boxes or extra legends.
- Overlap watch: distinct from integration/road comics because this is about operating-model alignment: skill, authorization/pairing, and throughput.

## Image Prompt

Create a square 2x2 editorial comic page in this exact visual style:

Hand-lettered workplace cartoon, minimalist black-and-white editorial comic strip, clean white background, pen-and-ink line art, light gray marker shading, thick slightly imperfect panel borders, simple stick-figure office characters with round heads and expressive but spare faces, slightly more rendered grayscale AI vehicles, dry professional satire, readable signage, sparse speech bubbles, uncluttered composition, near-monochrome black-white-gray palette with only tiny muted warning accents if useful.

Overall page layout:
- Large hand-lettered title across the top: "MISALIGNMENT SCALES TRAFFIC"
- Four equal panels in a 2x2 grid.
- Each panel has a black square number badge in the upper-left corner: "1.", "2.", "3.", "4."
- Each panel has one clear handwritten narrator caption near the top.
- Keep all text large and legible.
- Use only the exact text specified below.
- Add a centered two-line bottom kicker below the panels.
- Add a small artist signature at the bottom-right corner: "KKLL"

Global speech bubble rules:
- Every speech bubble must sit above or immediately beside the person speaking.
- Every speech bubble must have a clear short tail pointing directly to that speaker's mouth.
- Bubble tails must not cross another person, another bubble, the vehicle, the gate, the road, or major props.
- Do not leave any speech bubble floating without an obvious speaker.
- Do not attach a speech bubble to the wrong person.
- Keep dialogue bubbles compact, with large readable hand lettering.

Panel 1:
Narrator caption: "Capability + Capacity, No Access"
Scene: A capable AI engineer with a laptop bag stands beside a practical grayscale car labeled "AI Use Case." The engineer looks ready to drive. Behind the locked gate is a visibly open multi-lane road with plenty of room, a completed bridge or merge lane, and a sign labeled "Scale." This road capacity should look available and ready. But the car is blocked by a locked gate, badge reader, or dispatch booth labeled "Access Required." A gatekeeper stands inside the booth with a clipboard. Make the missing access visually obvious while capability and capacity are both visually present.
Labels: "AI Use Case", "Scale", "Access Required"
Speech bubbles:
Engineer: "We can build this."
Gatekeeper: "This team isn't assigned to the route."
Small bottom line inside panel: "Right driver. Road ready. No keys."
Mood: competent team and available capacity blocked by decision rights.

Panel 2:
Narrator caption: "Capability + Access, No Capacity"
Scene: The practical grayscale car labeled "AI Use Case" has a visible windshield pass labeled "Access Pass" and is moving through an open gate or approved checkpoint onto a tiny one-lane ramp labeled "Pilot Lane." The ramp tries to merge onto a much larger road labeled "Scale," but the merge is unfinished and too narrow.
Critical visual requirement: Show the same engineer twice in the same panel, clearly as the same person. The driver inside the car and the road-crew worker outside the car must have the same face, same hair, same shirt, same tie, and matching "AI Eng" badges. The driver is at the wheel. The identical road-crew version is in a construction vest trying to widen the lane with a shovel, cones, and a small roadwork patch. Use mirror-image or motion-line staging so the joke reads as one person doing both jobs, not two unrelated people.
Labels: "AI Use Case", "Access Pass", "Pilot Lane", "Scale"
Speech bubbles:
Driver-engineer: "We're approved and moving."
Road-crew engineer: "Also me: still building the lane."
Small bottom line inside panel: "Right driver. Access approved. Not enough lane."
Mood: approval and skill exist, throughput does not.

Panel 3:
Narrator caption: "Capacity + Access, No Capability"

The vehicle is stalled at the starting line with a tiny puff from the hood or a dashboard warning. A manager nearby gestures proudly at the open road. An observer stands to the side. Labels: "Scale", "Route Pass", "Intro to AI"
Speech bubbles:
Manager: "We gave it runway."
Observer: "Not a driver for this vehicle."

Mood: sponsorship and route permission without the skill to operate.

Executive: "Why so many projects and so few outcomes?"
Advisor: "We scaled traffic, not the operating model."

Bottom kicker centered below panels:
"Misalignment scales traffic, not outcomes."

Strict style constraints:
- Monochrome black-and-white only, with light grayscale shading.
- Keep humans as simple stick figures; make vehicles, gates, road signs, and highways slightly more detailed.
- Keep all lettering large, hand-drawn, and readable.
- Do not add extra invented text.
- Do not overcrowd the panels.
- Do not add a dense dashboard, status table, or infographic legend.
- The captions must be exactly: "Capability + Capacity, No Access"; "Capability + Access, No Capacity"; "Capacity + Access, No Capability"; "Enterprise scale".
- Panel 2 must clearly show the driver and road-crew worker as the same engineer with matching clothes and badges.
- The critique should target enterprise operating-model design, not make employees look stupid.
- Avoid real AI vendor logos or direct branding.
- Do not make the image glossy, colorful, cinematic, photorealistic, anime-inspired, superhero-inspired, or sci-fi.
