# Comic 005 Sketch Prompt V1: Reference Style

This prompt turns the guardrails security analogy into a production-oriented comic: the ordinary approved route with guardrails reaches the destination, while the fast unguarded shortcut fails in the background.

## Image Prompt

Create a square 2x2 editorial comic page in this exact visual style:

Hand-lettered workplace cartoon, monochrome black-and-white pen-and-ink line art, clean white background, light gray marker shading, thick slightly imperfect panel borders, simple stick-figure office characters with round heads and spare expressions, more detailed grayscale road infrastructure, guardrails, signs, traffic cameras, ordinary cars, sleek prototype cars, dry professional satire, readable signage, sparse speech bubbles, uncluttered composition.

Overall page layout:
- Large hand-lettered title across the top: "The Guardrails Were The Road"
- Four equal panels in a 2x2 grid.
- Each panel has a black square number badge in the upper-left corner: "1.", "2.", "3.", "4."
- Each panel has one short handwritten narrator caption near the top.
- Keep all text large and legible.
- Use only the text specified below.
- Add a large centered bottom kicker below the panels.
- Add a small artist signature at the bottom-right corner: "TT + KKLL"

Panel 1:
Narrator caption: "Two teams picked a route."
Scene: A fork in the road. On the left, two modest ordinary AI cars enter a road with clear guardrails. On the right, sleek advanced cars point toward a narrow cliffside shortcut with no guardrails. Keep the cars simple but visibly different.
Left road sign: "Approved AI Route"
Guardrail label: "Security Requirements"
Right road sign: "Cliffside Shortcut"
Sleek car label: "Advanced AI"
Speech bubble from fancy driver: "We'll be faster."
Mood: tempting shortcut versus boring safe road.

Panel 2:
Narrator caption: "The approved road had rules."
Scene: The ordinary cars drive calmly along the guarded route. Guardrails run along both sides. Add one traffic camera and one small trip-log booth or clipboard stand.
Road signs:
"NO PII"
"NO SECRETS"
"Monitoring"
"Trip Log"
Speech bubble from passenger: "This route feels slower."
Mood: mild impatience, but the route is clearly engineered.

Panel 3:
Narrator caption: "The shortcut looked efficient."
Scene: The sleek cars speed along a bare cliffside road. The road has no guardrails, no shoulder, and a broken or absent camera pole. Show speed lines, but keep the panel clean.
Road sign: "Shortcut to Production"
Warning sign: "NO GUARDRAILS"
Small sign near broken camera pole: "No monitoring"
Mood: fast, confident, visibly unsafe.

Panel 4:
Narrator caption: "Production was on the guarded route."
Scene: Foreground: the ordinary cars arrive safely at a destination sign. Drivers look around with dry confusion. Far in the background, on a distant cliff road, tiny sleek cars have gone off the unguarded route, shown with dust puffs, skid marks, and broken road fragments. Keep the cliff gag small and nonviolent.
Destination sign: "Production Value"
Speech bubble from ordinary driver: "Where did the fast team go?"
Mood: deadpan consequence, not disaster spectacle.

Bottom kicker centered below panels:
"The guardrails were not the delay. They were the road."

Signature:
"TT + KKLL"

Strict style constraints:
- Monochrome black-and-white only, with light grayscale shading.
- Keep humans as simple stick figures; make cars, guardrails, signs, traffic cameras, and road geometry slightly more detailed.
- Security should read as production road design, not bureaucracy.
- Use one clean visual absurdity per panel.
- Do not make security look like the villain.
- Do not make the cliff scene violent or chaotic.
- Do not add extra invented text.
- Do not overcrowd the panels.
- Keep all lettering large, hand-drawn, and readable.
