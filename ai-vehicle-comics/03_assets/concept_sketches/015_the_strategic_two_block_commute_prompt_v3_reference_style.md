# Comic 015 Sketch Prompt V3: Reference Style

It shifts the title from the literal "Formula 1 Cars For Two-Block Commutes" to the more sarcastic "The Strategic Two-Block Commute," simplifies Panel 2 to one visible race car, makes Panel 3's parked-but-burning metaphor more physical with rear tires lifted off the ground, and lets Panel 4 land as a dry boardroom punchline instead of explaining the cost.

## Pre-Production Design Check

- Core message: Premium model access without judgment, enablement, model tiering, cost controls, and monitoring turns enterprise strategy into fuel credits.
- Causal chain: leadership hands out top-tier model keys as a prestige moment; users treat tiny tasks like high-performance trips; sessions keep burning after the task appears parked; the bill arrives and users still ask for more fuel.
- Visual contrast: unrealistic excitement over a Formula 1 key versus the deadpan fact of a two-block commute.
- Panel logic: key excitement creates the mismatch, the simplified commute plan shows status-driven overuse, the lifted spinning tires show hidden ongoing burn, and the invoice plus fuel-credit request delivers the consequence.
- Final consequence: the $500M bill is earned by prestige-driven access and unattended burn, but Panel 4 does not explain it aloud.
- Punchline: "They asked for more fuel credits..." is dry because it arrives after the $500M number without adding a lecture.
- Clutter risk: one visible race car in Panel 2, three people in Panel 3, and only one speech bubble in Panel 4.
- Overlap watch: distinct from license-adoption comics because this is specifically premium model prestige, weak tiering, and ongoing cost burn.

## Image Prompt

Create a square 2x2 editorial comic page in this exact visual style:

Hand-lettered workplace cartoon, minimalist black-and-white editorial comic strip, clean white background, pen-and-ink line art, light gray marker shading, thick slightly imperfect panel borders, simple stick-figure office characters with round heads and expressive but spare faces, slightly more rendered grayscale Formula 1 cars and one practical sedan, dry professional satire, readable signage, sparse speech bubbles, uncluttered composition, near-monochrome black-white-gray palette with only tiny muted warning accents if useful.

Overall page layout:
- Large hand-lettered title across the top: "THE STRATEGIC TWO-BLOCK COMMUTE"
- Four equal panels in a 2x2 grid.
- Each panel has a black square number badge in the upper-left corner: "1.", "2.", "3.", "4."
- Each panel has one short handwritten narrator caption near the top.
- Keep all text large and legible.
- Use only the text specified below.
- Add a centered bottom kicker below the panels.
- Add a small artist signature at the bottom-right corner: "KKLL"

Global speech bubble rules:
- Every speech bubble must sit above or immediately beside the person speaking.
- Every speech bubble must have a clear short tail pointing directly to that speaker's mouth.
- Bubble tails must not cross another person, another bubble, the AI key, the car, or major props.
- Do not leave any speech bubble floating without an obvious speaker.
- Do not attach a speech bubble to the wrong person.
- Keep dialogue bubbles compact, with large readable hand lettering.

Panel 1:
Narrator caption: "The key handoff."
Scene: Corporate office-campus launch event in a parking lot. Make the visual comparison dramatic and sarcastic: in the foreground, a wildly excited employee receives an AI key from a calm executive. The employee receiving the key has unrealistic expectations: huge grin, stars or dramatic excitement lines, leaning forward like this changes everything. A side watcher stands at the right with a deadpan expression. Behind them is a row of sleek grayscale Formula 1 cars like a corporate product launch. Off to the side is a plain gray sedan labeled "Standard Copilot".
Banner: "PREMIUM AI ACCESS"
Speech bubbles:
Executive: "Your top-tier model key."
Excited employee: "At last. Strategic velocity."
Side watcher: "Dude, you live just two blocks away..."
Mood: corporate prestige meeting tiny actual need.

Panel 2:

Scene: Simplified office parking lot with only one visible Formula 1 race car. The race car dominates the center, labeled "Top-Tier Model". One driver is already in the race car, yelling with excitement. Another employee with an AI key walks toward the racing car area, thinking about a tiny errand. On the side, a third person stands next to a plain sedan labeled "Standard Copilot" and looks disappointed because the sedan is practical but not trendy.
Important simplification: Show only one race car in this panel. Do not show multiple race cars, a pit crew, printer, mailroom, or many errands.
Speech bubbles:

Key holder thought bubble: "Supercharged grocery run!"
Sedan person: "It doesn't feel enterprise."
Labels: "Top-Tier Model", "Standard Copilot"
Mood: tiny trip, enormous horsepower, status anxiety.

Panel 3:
Narrator caption: "Parked for the night."
Scene: End of day outside the office. One Formula 1 car is parked but still active. Its rear end is raised by a wedge-like maintenance machine or angled lift, so the two rear tires are visibly hanging in the air and not touching the ground. Both rear tires spin ferociously with motion blur and smoke. The front of the car is still on the ground. The AI key is still in the car. A small token/fuel gauge is in the red.
Three people only:
- Driver on the far left, walking away with a briefcase, leaving for the night.
- IT technician near the rear tires, looking confused and pointing at the spinning tires.
- Business-suit manager on the far right, calm and confident.
Labels/monitor text: "Premium model session active", "Fuel burn: ACTIVE"
Speech bubbles:
Driver: "All set. I parked it."
IT technician: "Why are the rear tires still spinning?"
Business suit: "Look, it's still creating value."
Visual rule: The two rear tires must be clearly off the ground and spinning in the air. The wedge machinery must visibly hold the back end up. This is the core metaphor.
Mood: dry absurdity, not slapstick.

Panel 4:
Narrator caption: "The fuel meeting."
Scene: Executive board room. People are shocked and puzzled while staring at a huge screen or invoice. One person covers her eyes and does not want to look at the number. Another person leans back stunned. An IT person stands at the conference room door holding a clipboard or laptop, reporting in. Do not explain why the number happened. Let the image and short line be the punchline.
Invoice text large: "MONTHLY AI FUEL CHARGES: $500,000,000"
Speech bubble from IT person at the door: "They asked for more fuel credits..."
Composition rule: The $500,000,000 number must be the largest text in the panel after the title. The IT person at the door must be clearly the speaker. Show shocked and puzzled boardroom reactions, including one person covering her eyes.
Mood: dry, dramatic, restrained boardroom horror.

Bottom kicker centered below panels:
"Strategy, apparently, was fuel credits."

Strict style constraints:
- Monochrome black-and-white only, with light grayscale shading.
- Keep humans as simple stick figures; make vehicles, telemetry screens, invoice, and office signage slightly more detailed.
- The critique should target leadership and operating-model design, not make employees look stupid.
- Avoid real AI vendor logos or direct branding.
- Do not make the image glossy, colorful, cinematic, photorealistic, anime-inspired, superhero-inspired, or sci-fi.
- Do not add extra invented text.
- Do not overcrowd the panels.
- Keep all lettering large, hand-drawn, and readable.
