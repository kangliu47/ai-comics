# Comic 003 Style Comparison Notes

This comparison uses the same core idea from Comic 003, "Waiting for the Cybertruck," but changes the artistic posture.

## Current Editorial Minimalist

File: `003_waiting_for_the_cybertruck_style_comparison_current_editorial.png`

This version reads like a clean workplace editorial cartoon. It preserves the 2x2 comic grammar: title, panel numbers, narrator captions, sparse dialogue, and a bottom kicker. The cars and road are readable, but the scene still feels like a small narrative with people waiting, one person acting, and the result proving the point.

Strengths:

- Fast to understand.
- Friendly enough for a business audience.
- The vehicle analogy is concrete and legible.
- The final contrast is clear without feeling harsh.

Tradeoff:

- The mockery is relatively polite.
- The humans still carry much of the scene.
- The absurdity feels observational rather than structurally biting.

## First-Principles Deadpan

File: `003_waiting_for_the_cybertruck_style_comparison_first_principles_deadpan.png`

This version treats the comic more like a whiteboard proof of enterprise irrationality. The parking lot, road, arrows, dashboard, signs, and labels carry the joke. Humans are smaller and more schematic. The humor comes from a calm system diagram showing that a working path from task to done already exists while leaders solemnly wait for a more impressive future object.

Strengths:

- Sharper mockery of hype and institutional self-satisfaction.
- More absurd without becoming random slapstick.
- The flawed premise is more visible as a causal mechanism.
- The objects and labels do more of the satirical work.

Tradeoff:

- Slightly colder and less character-driven.
- More dependent on precise labels and diagram composition.
- Less warm as a general workplace cartoon.

## Editorial Assessment

The old style says: "Here is a funny enterprise AI behavior, shown as a vehicle cartoon."

The First-Principles Deadpan style says: "Here is the broken premise reduced to a diagram, and the people are somehow proud of it."

For this editorial direction, First-Principles Deadpan is the stronger canonical style. It better supports increased absurdity and mockery while preserving the compact vehicle analogy. The key guardrail is to keep the absurdity causal: the final panel should reveal the consequence of a bad premise, not just add visual chaos.
