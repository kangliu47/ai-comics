# Comic 016 Sketch Prompt V1: Reference Style

This prompt turns the operating-model alignment concept into a reference-style visual draft. It preserves the core message: AI use cases only scale when capability, access, and capacity align around the right use case.

## Pre-Production Design Check

- Core message: AI use cases scale when capability, access, and capacity are aligned around the right use cases.
- Causal chain: enterprises launch pilots with one pillar missing; each pilot creates local activity but cannot reach scaled outcomes; multiplying those pilots creates enterprise traffic.
- Visual contrast: individual pilots with one missing operating-model pillar versus a clean highway sign for enterprise outcomes.
- Panel logic: blocked access, constrained capacity, missing capability, then accumulated enterprise traffic.
- Final consequence: the highway jam is earned because each prior panel contributes a different stuck AI vehicle to the enterprise system.
- Punchline: "Misalignment scales traffic, not outcomes" names the operating-model failure without turning into generic "AI bad" humor.
- Clutter risk: each panel gets one missing-pillar visual, one caption, one dialogue pair, and only the labels needed for comprehension.
- Overlap watch: distinct from road/integration comics because the primary failure is operating-model alignment, not missing APIs or roads.

## Image Prompt

Create a square 2x2 editorial comic page in this exact visual style:

Hand-lettered workplace cartoon, minimalist black-and-white editorial comic strip, clean white background, pen-and-ink line art, light gray marker shading, thick slightly imperfect panel borders, simple stick-figure office characters with round heads and expressive but spare faces, slightly more rendered grayscale AI vehicles, dry professional satire, readable signage, sparse speech bubbles, uncluttered composition, near-monochrome black-white-gray palette with only tiny muted warning accents if useful.

Overall page layout:
- Large hand-lettered title across the top: "MISALIGNMENT SCALES TRAFFIC"
- Four equal panels in a 2x2 grid.
- Each panel has a black square number badge in the upper-left corner: "1.", "2.", "3.", "4."
- Each panel has one short handwritten narrator caption near the top.
- Keep all text large and legible.
- Use only the exact text specified below.
- Add a centered two-line bottom kicker below the panels.
- Add a small artist signature at the bottom-right corner: "KKLL"

Global speech bubble rules:
- Every speech bubble must sit above or immediately beside the person speaking.
- Every speech bubble must have a clear short tail pointing directly to that speaker's mouth.
- Bubble tails must not cross another person, another bubble, the vehicle, the gate, the road, or major props.
- Do not leave any speech bubble floating without an obvious speaker.
- Do not attach a speech bubble to the wrong person.
- Keep dialogue bubbles compact, with large readable hand lettering.

Panel 1:
Narrator caption: "Capability without access"
Scene: A capable AI engineer with a laptop bag stands beside a practical grayscale car labeled "AI Use Case." The engineer looks ready to drive. A clear road beyond the gate points toward a sign labeled "Scale." But the car is blocked by a locked gate, badge reader, or dispatch booth labeled "Access Required." A gatekeeper stands inside the booth with a clipboard. Make the missing access visually obvious: the right driver and vehicle are present, but the route is locked.
Labels: "AI Use Case", "Scale", "Access Required"
Speech bubbles:
Engineer: "We can build this."
Gatekeeper: "This team isn't assigned to the route."
Small bottom line inside panel: "Right driver. No keys."
Mood: competent team blocked by decision rights, not tool failure.

Panel 2:
Narrator caption: "Access without capacity"
Scene: The same practical grayscale car labeled "AI Use Case" is now approved and moving on a tiny one-lane ramp labeled "Pilot Lane." The ramp is trying to merge onto a much larger road labeled "Scale." Show the absurdity clearly: the same engineer appears twice in the panel, once driving the car and once in a road-crew vest trying to widen the lane with a shovel, cone, or roadwork sign. Do not make the panel busy; one tiny lane and one road-crew version of the engineer are enough.
Labels: "AI Use Case", "Pilot Lane", "Scale"
Speech bubbles:
Driver-engineer: "We're approved and moving."
Road-crew engineer: "If I can finish the lane."
Small bottom line inside panel: "Right driver. Right car. Not enough lane."
Mood: approval exists, throughput does not.

Panel 3:
Narrator caption: "Capacity without capability"
Scene: A wide open multi-lane road labeled "Scale" stretches forward with no traffic. A practical grayscale AI car has a pass on the windshield labeled "Route Pass." The assigned driver sits in the car looking uncertain while holding a manual labeled "Intro to AI." The vehicle is stalled at the starting line with a tiny puff from the hood or a dashboard warning. A manager nearby gestures proudly at the open road. An observer stands to the side. The critique should be assignment mismatch, not personal stupidity.
Labels: "Scale", "Route Pass", "Intro to AI"
Speech bubbles:
Manager: "We gave it runway."
Observer: "Not a driver for this vehicle."
Small bottom line inside panel: "Plenty of road. Wrong driver."
Mood: sponsored capacity without the skill to operate.

Panel 4:
Narrator caption: "Enterprise scale"
Scene: Wide overhead highway-system view of the enterprise road network. Show several small AI vehicles scattered across the roads in different stuck states: one stopped at an access gate, one inching along a tiny pilot lane, one stalled with a confused driver, one waiting at an unfinished bridge, one duplicated car labeled "Rebuilt Version," one labeled "Team Bot," and one labeled "POC." In the distance, a clean multi-lane highway sign points to "Enterprise Outcomes," but most cars are not reaching it. Keep the view legible and editorial, not chaotic or dense.
Labels: "Enterprise Outcomes", "Team Bot", "POC", "Rebuilt Version"
Speech bubbles:
Executive: "Why so many projects and so few outcomes?"
Advisor: "We scaled traffic, not the operating model."
Mood: systemic scaling failure, not random chaos.

Bottom kicker centered below panels:
"Misalignment scales traffic, not outcomes."
"The right operating model turns pilots into a highway."

Signature:
"KKLL"

Strict style constraints:
- Monochrome black-and-white only, with light grayscale shading.
- Keep humans as simple stick figures; make vehicles, gates, road signs, and highways slightly more detailed.
- Keep all lettering large, hand-drawn, and readable.
- Do not add extra invented text.
- Do not overcrowd the panels.
- Do not add a dense dashboard, status table, or infographic legend.
- The optional pillar status boxes should be omitted unless necessary for clarity.
- The critique should target enterprise operating-model design, not make employees look stupid.
- Avoid real AI vendor logos or direct branding.
- Do not make the image glossy, colorful, cinematic, photorealistic, anime-inspired, superhero-inspired, or sci-fi.
