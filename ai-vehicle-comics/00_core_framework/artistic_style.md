# Artistic Style

## Chosen Direction

Minimalist monochrome stick-figure editorial comic, dry professional satire, clean pen-and-ink line work, light grayscale shading, highly legible visual metaphor.

## Desired Look

- Very simple monochrome or near-monochrome pen-and-ink comic.
- Minimalist stick-figure or lightly rendered human characters.
- Clean black hand-drawn outlines.
- White background with light grayscale shading only when needed.
- Flat 2D comic framing.
- Clear panel borders.
- Expressive but simple facial expressions.
- Witty editorial-cartoon feel.
- Understated and clever rather than polished, cinematic, or flashy.

## Reference Style Variant

For stronger visual drafts, prefer a square or near-square 2x2 editorial comic page:

- Large hand-lettered title across the top.
- Four panels in a 2x2 grid with thick, slightly imperfect borders.
- Black square panel-number badges in the upper-left corner of each panel.
- One short narrator caption at the top of each panel.
- Sparse speech bubbles for character-level comedy.
- A centered bottom kicker outside the panels for the final takeaway.
- Stick-figure workplace characters paired with a slightly more rendered grayscale vehicle.
- Mostly black, white, and gray, with tiny muted warning accents only when they improve readability.

## Avoid

- Glossy, colorful, poster-like rendering.
- Hyper-detailed vehicle design.
- Anime-inspired character styling.
- Faux photorealism.
- Busy backgrounds.
- Dense labels.
- Overly cinematic camera angles.
- Sci-fi worldbuilding that distracts from enterprise satire.

## Visual Priorities

- The joke should be understandable in 3-5 seconds.
- Text should be short and legible.
- The vehicle metaphor should be readable instantly.
- Signs, banners, dashboards, and a few labels are useful, but should not crowd the strip.
- Humor should come from the situation and contrast, not visual chaos.
- The final panel should often deliver the sharpest absurd consequence.
