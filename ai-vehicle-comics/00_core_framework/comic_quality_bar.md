# Comic Quality Bar

Use this checklist when drafting, revising, or selecting comic concepts.

## Concept

- The comic has one clear enterprise AI insight.
- The vehicle metaphor clarifies the idea instead of decorating it.
- The joke is not just "AI bad" or "enterprise dumb"; it names a specific failure mode.
- The concept is distinct from existing backlog items.

## Structure

- Four panels are preferred unless a different layout clearly improves the idea.
- Panel 1 establishes the ordinary enterprise setup.
- Panels 2-3 reveal the contradiction or escalation.
- Panel 4 delivers the absurd consequence or cleanest punchline.
- The final panel should be stronger than the caption.

## Pre-Production Check

- The causal chain is clear before generating an image.
- The visual consequence follows from the setup instead of adding random drama.
- The main contrast can be described in one sentence.
- The panel design has been tightened at the text level before drawing.
- The image prompt should express a solved design, not discover the design.

## Text

- Dialogue is short enough to read quickly.
- Labels are used only where they sharpen comprehension.
- The punchline is specific, not generic.
- The comic should not require a paragraph of explanation below it.

## Visuals

- Composition remains clean in monochrome.
- Characters are simple but expressive.
- The key object, usually the vehicle, range meter, map, road, ramp, sign, or dashboard, reads instantly.
- Visual sarcasm should do some of the work.

## Enterprise Accuracy

- The critique maps to a real 2026 enterprise AI issue.
- Security comics distinguish user error, governance failure, and adversarial behavior.
- Security comics distinguish preventive requirements, restricted-data boundaries, logging/monitoring, auditability, and vendor retention standards.
- Adoption comics distinguish procurement, access, training, workflow fit, and behavior change.
- Pilot rollout comics distinguish license access, tool fit, token/range cost, data/map quality, integration/route access, and measurable outcomes.
- Agentic comics preserve the importance of monitoring, guardrails, verification, and takeover.

## Revision Questions

- Can one label be removed without losing the idea?
- Can the punchline be more specific?
- Is this a new comic, or a variant of an existing one?
- Does the vehicle analogy still fit naturally?
- Is the final consequence causally earned by the previous panels?
- Are tokens, data, and integrations mapped to distinct visual roles instead of blurred together?
- Is each security visual doing specific work, such as guardrails, "NO PII" signs, traffic cameras, trip logs, or no-retention vendor signage?
- Does the comic avoid making security the villain when the real issue is poor road design?
- Would a business leader recognize the behavior being mocked?
