# Series Thesis

Modern AI adoption is often discussed as if the only question is engine power: which model is newest, fastest, largest, cheapest, or most agentic.

That framing misses the enterprise reality. A powerful engine does not create useful transportation by itself. People need vehicles they can operate, enough energy for the trip, trusted maps, roads to useful destinations, rules of the road, security guardrails, monitoring, maintenance, and the ability to intervene when automation goes wrong.

## Editorial Position

The series should expose the absurd gap between enterprise AI ambition and operational readiness.

It should be skeptical of:

- Model worship.
- License-procurement theater.
- Agentic hype without enablement.
- Token waste disguised as "advanced usage."
- Data-foundation neglect that leaves the autopilot navigating with bad maps.
- Integration wishful thinking.
- Governance performed after the pileup.
- Security requirements treated as a final sign-off instead of guardrails built into the road.
- Vendor AI standards that ignore whether company data remains parked after the trip.
- Security teams blamed for failures caused by unusable official paths.

## Recurring Argument

Enterprise AI success is less about buying a more impressive vehicle and more about building a transportation system people can actually use: trained drivers, enough range, reliable maps, open roads, visible traffic rules, security guardrails, monitoring, and vendor routes that do not retain company data.
