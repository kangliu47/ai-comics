# Product Orchestration Analogy

Use this extension for comics about the failure mode where AI makes experimentation cheaper, but the enterprise scales fragmentation instead of capability.

## Core Insight

Lower experimentation cost is not the same as enterprise AI progress. Without product orchestration, every department can build a locally impressive vehicle, but the organization still lacks shared routes, reusable parts, maintenance plans, support models, ownership, prioritization, and a roadmap for turning pilots into production capabilities.

## Product Orchestration Map

| Enterprise AI Concept | Vehicle Analogy | Use In Comics |
|---|---|---|
| AI Product Lead | Fleet strategist, dispatcher, and product catalog owner | Connects local experimentation to enterprise capability. |
| Product orchestration | Fleet strategy and route planning | Decides what should be standardized, scaled, combined, retired, or left as a learning artifact. |
| AI prototypes and pilots | Concept vehicles and test drives | Useful for learning, not automatically production-ready. |
| Reusable patterns | Shared chassis, engines, parts, and maintenance procedures | Prevents every team from rebuilding the same capability. |
| Intake triage | Service desk, route permit, or drive-through order screen | Sorts requests before scarce technical teams start building. |
| Value scoring and sequencing | Traffic control and dispatch priority | Routes work by enterprise leverage, dependencies, and capacity. |
| Citizen development boundaries | Local driving rules and permitted vehicle classes | Lets teams move quickly where appropriate without creating unsupported machinery. |
| Product ownership | Vehicle registration, named owner, support model, and lifecycle record | Makes capabilities inspectable, maintainable, and retireable. |
| Data Science capacity | Expert pit crew for high-performance decision systems | Should be protected for ambiguous, high-value, decision-heavy work. |

## Distinguish Product Orchestration From Governance

Governance answers: is this safe, compliant, observable, and controlled?

Product orchestration answers: should we build this, in what form, for whom, in what order, with what reusable pattern, and how does it become an enterprise capability?

The two should appear together in some comics, but they are not the same joke. Governance is traffic safety. Product orchestration is fleet strategy, dispatch, product catalog discipline, and lifecycle ownership.

## Product Orchestration Failure Modes

- Car show: every department unveils a different custom AI vehicle for the same commute.
- Drive-through: the loudest AI orders hit the kitchen before anyone defines a product menu.
- Parking lot: demos technically worked, but no one productized, integrated, supported, or reused them.
- Horn volume: prioritization follows seniority, demo shine, or request noise instead of enterprise leverage.
- Steering wheels: democratization gives everyone controls without lanes, routes, or escalation rules.
- Parts bin: teams assemble AI components quickly while accumulating fragile operational debt.
- Roadside assistance: scarce Data Science capacity is consumed debugging disconnected prototypes.
- Convoy: teams move fast in different directions and leadership mistakes motion for alignment.
- Custom keys: the organization accumulates access without ownership, support, or lifecycle logic.
- Shortcut factory: locally rational shortcuts become an enterprise maze.

- "Innovation by horn volume was not in the strategy deck."
- "Access to parts is not the same as a vehicle strategy."
- "Every shortcut was rational. The maze was the strategy failure."

## Visual Guidance

Keep these comics visually ordinary. The absurdity should come from the enterprise operating model, not from exotic vehicles. Use boring corporate parking lots, service counters, garages, intersections, dispatch boards, route maps, maintenance clipboards, and standardized fleet catalogs.

The Product Lead should read as product-minded and operationally calm: clipboard, dispatch board, route map, fleet catalog, or decision matrix. Avoid making the Product Lead a heroic savior. The joke should be that the missing product function was obvious only after the organization manufactured chaos.
