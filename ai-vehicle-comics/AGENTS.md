# AGENTS.md

Durable instructions for future Codex sessions working on this project.

## Project Identity

This is a content-development and visual-storytelling project, not primarily a software engineering project. Preserve the comic-strip series as concise enterprise AI satire built around a compact vehicle analogy.

## Public-content boundary

Follow the root [privacy rules](../AGENTS.md#public-content-privacy-rules) for every artifact. All organizational examples are fictional composites. Before generation, include an identity/context check in the design pass; before selection, inspect artwork text and provenance and update the reviewed-image manifest. Keep initials without identity mappings.

## Non-Negotiables

- Preserve the core analogy: models are engines, products are vehicles, tokens are fuel/energy/range, data foundations are trusted maps and navigation information, integrations are roads/bridges/ramps, enablement is driver's education, oversight is taking the wheel, governance is traffic safety, and security requirements are roadway guardrails, signs, monitoring, and retention controls.
- Do not over-expand this into an elaborate fictional world, mascot universe, or glossy sci-fi franchise.
- Prioritize sharp enterprise AI satire over volume.
- Keep comics concise, legible, and understandable in 3-5 seconds.
- Avoid hype language. The point is to expose absurdity in enterprise AI behavior.
- Prefer wit, sarcasm, and clarity over dense explanation.
- Preserve the chosen visual direction: minimalist monochrome stick-figure editorial comic, clean pen-and-ink line work, light grayscale shading, highly legible visual metaphor.

## Audience

Write for technically literate business leaders, enterprise technology professionals, AI enablement teams, security leaders, and product-minded executives.

## Comic Proposal Format

When proposing or revising a comic concept, separate:

1. Message
2. Analogy
3. Four-panel beat
4. Dialogue
5. Punchline options
6. Image-generation notes
7. Open questions or risks

## Creative Standards

- Challenge weak metaphors instead of merely polishing them.
- Suggest stronger punchlines when a line is too generic.
- Identify overlap between concepts before creating near-duplicates.
- Keep the vehicle analogy compact and consistent.
- Use short signage, banners, dashboard labels, and workplace labels only when they help comprehension.
- Avoid clutter and excessive exposition.
- Make the final panel deliver a sharper absurd consequence than the setup.
- Before generating or revising visual drafts, run a text-level pre-production design check: core message, causal chain, visual contrast, panel logic, final consequence, punchline, clutter risk, and overlap watch.

## 2026 Relevance

Keep the series connected to real enterprise AI conversations:

- Copilot adoption gaps
- AI enablement and AI literacy
- Agentic hype
- Foundational data quality and access
- Enterprise workflow integrations
- Agent governance and observability
- AI security requirements, logging, monitoring, and vendor zero-data-retention standards
- Prompt injection and indirect instruction attacks
- Shadow AI
- Agent sprawl

## Visual Drafting Guidance

When generating image prompts, start from the master style language in `02_prompt_library/visual_style_master_prompt.md`. Favor simple black-and-white editorial strips with clear panel borders, spare characters, readable text, and no glossy cinematic finish.

Before image generation, use `00_core_framework/pre_production_design_check.md` to confirm the idea is actually ready to draw. Prefer improving the text design before asking the image model to solve unclear causality.
