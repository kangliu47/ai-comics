# Comic 024: The AI Parts Bin

Fictional industry scenario. People, organizations, workflows, and figures are illustrative.

## Status

Backlog concept for the product orchestration arc

## Core Message

Access to AI components accelerates assembly, but without product orchestration the enterprise accumulates fragile, redundant, and unsupported machinery.

## Core Critique

Teams rummage through copilots, APIs, prompts, workflows, connectors, dashboards, agents, and low-code components. Some assemblies are useful, some redundant, some impossible to support, and some become technical and operational debt disguised as speed.

## Vehicle Analogy

The enterprise has a giant AI parts bin. The AI Product Lead turns it into a product catalog with approved patterns, reusable components, known use cases, build-versus-buy guidance, and escalation paths.

## Audience

Platform teams, product leads, citizen development sponsors, architecture teams, and business teams assembling AI workflows from available tools.

## Candidate Four-Panel Flow

1. Teams stand around a huge bin labeled "AI Parts" filled with prompts, APIs, connectors, dashboards, agents, and low-code pieces.
2. Each team bolts together a different odd vehicle. Some move; some smoke; two are the same vehicle with different names.
3. Support asks for maintenance manuals. Everyone holds up a different sticky note.
4. The Product Lead sorts the bin into shelves labeled "Approved patterns," "Reusable components," "Known use cases," and "Escalate."

## Dialogue And Labels

- Panel 1: Sign: "AI Parts Bin."
- Panel 2: Team: "It only needs three manual steps."
- Panel 3: Support: "Which parts are approved?"
- Panel 4: Caption: "The parts bin became a product catalog."

## Punchline Options

- Access to parts is not the same as a vehicle strategy.
- We accelerated assembly and called the pile a platform.
- Reuse starts when the parts stop being a bin.

## Image-Generation Notes

Make the parts visually simple: boxes labeled "API," "Prompt," "Connector," "Agent," and "Dashboard." Do not create an unreadable junkyard. The final catalog shelves should feel orderly and a little boring.

## Open Questions

- Should the parts bin include brand names, or stay generic for durability?
- Should the final catalog be a shelf, menu board, or fleet manual?
