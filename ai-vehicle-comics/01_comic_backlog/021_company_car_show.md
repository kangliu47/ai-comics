# Comic 021: The Company Car Show

Fictional industry scenario. People, organizations, workflows, and figures are illustrative.

## Status

Backlog concept for the product orchestration arc

## Core Message

When every department builds its own impressive AI vehicle, the enterprise can celebrate local creativity while still failing to create a shared transportation system.

## Core Critique

AI experimentation can produce many clever local artifacts: finance scooters, HR golf carts, legal armored vans, IR dashboard sedans, and compliance tricycles with warning lights. Without product orchestration, none share parts, routes, maintenance, safety standards, or reusable capability.

## Vehicle Analogy

Each department unveils a custom AI vehicle at an internal car show. The AI Product Lead turns the car show into a fleet strategy with common vehicle classes, shared engines, reusable components, prioritized routes, and a roadmap for production vehicles.

## Audience

Enterprise AI councils, analytics leaders, department heads, enablement teams, and product leads trying to connect local innovation to enterprise reuse.

## Candidate Four-Panel Flow

1. A banner reads "Internal AI Innovation Car Show." Departments proudly stand beside custom vehicles.
2. The vehicles are individually charming but incompatible: "Finance Scooter," "HR Golf Cart," "Legal Armored Van," and "Compliance Tricycle."
3. A mechanic asks where the shared parts, routes, maintenance plans, and safety standards are. Everyone points to a different binder.
4. The Product Lead replaces the prize table with a fleet strategy board: "Vehicle classes / Shared parts / Priority routes / Production roadmap."

## Dialogue And Labels

- Panel 1: Host: "Look at all this innovation."
- Panel 2: Vehicle labels: "Finance," "HR," "Legal," "Compliance."
- Panel 3: Mechanic: "Do any of these share parts?"
- Panel 4: Caption: "The car show became a fleet strategy."

## Punchline Options

- Congratulations. We invented twelve vehicles for the same commute.
- The company won the car show and lost the fleet plan.
- Local novelty is not enterprise mobility.

## Image-Generation Notes

Use a trade-show layout with small booths and department signs. The vehicles should be funny through mismatch, not visual chaos. Keep the Product Lead's final fleet board clean and boring in the best way.

## Open Questions

- Should this comic name real departments, or use generic "Team A / Team B" labels to keep it broadly reusable?
- Should the punchline use "twelve vehicles" or a smaller number visible in the strip?
