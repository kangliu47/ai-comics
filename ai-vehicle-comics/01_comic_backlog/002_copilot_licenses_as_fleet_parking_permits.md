# Comic 002: Keys Assigned, Nobody Driving

Fictional industry scenario. People, organizations, workflows, and figures are illustrative.

## Status

Revised concept; selected draft generated

## Core Message

Assigning AI licenses is not the same as adoption, enablement, or business value.

## Core Critique

Enterprises often turn AI rollout into a license-assignment KPI. Leadership can report that everyone has access, but employees still do not know which work trips are worth taking, how to use the tools well, or why the tools matter to their actual jobs.

## Vehicle Analogy

Copilot, ChatGPT, and internal assistants are vehicles in a corporate fleet. Licenses are key fobs or access badges, not driving skill. Adoption requires driver training, useful destinations, workflow fit, and a reason to get in the car.

## Candidate Four-Panel Flow

1. Leadership unveils an AI adoption KPI dashboard reading "100% licenses assigned" while handing identical key fobs to employees.
2. Inside the corporate garage, rows of parked cars are labeled "Copilot," "ChatGPT," "Internal Assistant," and "Meeting Summarizer." A small sign says "Driver training: TBD."
3. Employees walk on the sidewalk beside the garage, still holding key fobs. One asks: "Where are we supposed to go?" Another says: "Which one helps with my job?"
4. Leadership points proudly at the dashboard reading "10,000 keys issued." Behind them, the garage remains full and the employees are still walking.

## Dialogue And Labels

- Panel 1: Dashboard: "AI adoption KPI: 100% licenses assigned." Leader: "Everyone gets a key!"
- Panel 2: Car labels: "Copilot," "ChatGPT," "Internal Assistant." Small sign: "Driver training: TBD."
- Panel 3: Employee: "Where are we supposed to go?" Second employee: "Which one helps with my job?"
- Panel 4: Dashboard: "10,000 keys issued." Caption or leader: "Adoption complete."

## Punchline Options

- Keys assigned. Nobody driving.
- Access is not adoption.
- You bought the fleet. You forgot the driving school.

## Image-Generation Notes

Use the parking garage as the main visual metaphor, but make the key fobs/access badges more important than "licenses" as legal driving credentials. Keep cars simple and repeated, not detailed. Make the absurdity come from motionless abundance: many vehicles, no movement, and employees still walking outside with keys in hand.

## Revision Notes

- The selected direction uses "Adoption complete" as the dry dashboard joke and "Keys assigned. Nobody driving" as the bottom kicker.
- The visual prompt explicitly avoids a legal driver's license card so the license reads as enterprise software access, not driving certification.

## Premium Assistant Pilot Connection

In a hypothetical premium-assistant pilot variant, the garage contains Model Y key fobs requested by leaders, but the intake board still has blank fields for "Use case," "existing tool alternative," "Token budget," and "Success metric."

The sharper illustrative pilot joke is not that people want access. It is that access is being treated as the strategy before anyone has defined the trip.
