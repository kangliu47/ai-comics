# Comic 027: The Shortcut Factory

Fictional industry scenario. People, organizations, workflows, and figures are illustrative.

## Status

Backlog concept for the product orchestration arc

## Core Message

Local AI shortcuts can be rational in isolation while still creating an enterprise maze when no product function turns repeated pain into shared routes.

## Core Critique

AI makes it easy for teams to build small shortcut roads around local pain. Each shortcut solves something immediate, but the same destinations end up with many unofficial paths, none designed for long-term traffic, governance, support, or reuse.

## Vehicle Analogy

Every team builds its own shortcut road. The AI Product Lead treats repeated shortcuts as discovery signal and decides when the enterprise needs a real shared route.

## Audience

Enterprise AI leaders, product leads, architecture teams, governance partners, citizen development sponsors, and teams tempted by permanent local workarounds.

## Candidate Four-Panel Flow

1. A team builds a small shortcut road around a pothole labeled "Manual process."
2. Other teams build their own shortcuts to the same destination, each with different signs and surfaces.
3. The overhead view reveals a maze of unofficial roads crossing, dead-ending, and bypassing support checkpoints.
4. The Product Lead overlays a clean route map and marks the shortcuts as discovery signals for a real enterprise road.

## Dialogue And Labels

- Panel 1: Team: "This shortcut solves our problem."
- Panel 2: Signs: "Finance path," "HR path," "Legal path."
- Panel 3: Map label: "Enterprise route map."
- Panel 4: Product Lead: "Three shortcuts to the same place means we need a road."

## Punchline Options

- Every shortcut was rational. The maze was the strategy failure.
- The shortcuts worked until everyone took one.
- A workaround is a signal, not a transportation plan.

## Image-Generation Notes

The overhead maze is the key visual. Keep it simple enough to read: three or four shortcut roads, one shared destination, and a missing official road. The final panel should make the conversion from local workaround to product roadmap visible.
