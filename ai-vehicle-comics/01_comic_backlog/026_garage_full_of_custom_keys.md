# Comic 026: The Garage Full of Custom Keys

Fictional industry scenario. People, organizations, workflows, and figures are illustrative.

## Status

Backlog concept for the product orchestration arc

## Core Message

Enterprise AI access without product ownership creates a garage full of keys, quirks, and unclear accountability.

## Core Critique

Every AI tool, automation, and prototype develops its own key, owner, usage pattern, support model, and undocumented behavior. The enterprise accumulates access faster than it assigns lifecycle ownership or decides what deserves scale.

## Vehicle Analogy

AI capabilities are vehicles with custom keys. The AI Product Lead creates product ownership logic: who the capability serves, what problem it solves, what value it creates, who supports it, and when it should scale or retire.

## Audience

Product owners, analytics leaders, platform teams, governance partners, support teams, and executives trying to inventory AI work.

## Candidate Four-Panel Flow

1. A garage wall is covered in labeled keys: "Sales Bot," "Policy Assistant," "Ops Macro," "Forecast POC," and "Agent v2."
2. People argue over which keys still matter, which are duplicates, and which vehicles still start.
3. A support person opens one garage door to reveal a vehicle with no owner, no manual, and a sticky note reading "Ask the owner."
4. The Product Lead replaces the key wall with a product registry: "User / Problem / Value / Owner / Support / Retire date."

## Dialogue And Labels

- Panel 1: Sign: "AI Capability Keys."
- Panel 2: Team: "Is this one still used?"
- Panel 3: Sticky note: "Ask the owner."
- Panel 4: Caption: "We had keys to everything except the roadmap."

## Punchline Options

- We had keys to everything except the roadmap.
- Access multiplied faster than ownership.
- The key rack was not a product portfolio.

## Image-Generation Notes

Use the key wall as the main visual. Keep labels short and avoid dozens of keys. The final product registry should be legible and structured. The "Ask the owner" note is a useful enterprise-specific joke if it does not clutter the panel.

## Open Questions

- Should the punchline use "roadmap" or "owner" for sharper product emphasis?
- Should this comic connect to Copilot license keys, or stay broader across tools and prototypes?
