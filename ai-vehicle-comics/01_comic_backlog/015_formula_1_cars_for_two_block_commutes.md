# Comic 015: The Strategic Two-Block Commute

Fictional industry scenario. People, organizations, workflows, and figures are illustrative.

## Status

Selected visual draft v3 created

## Core Message

Premium model access without judgment, enablement, model tiering, usage norms, cost controls, and monitoring turns enterprise strategy into fuel credits.

## Core Critique

Enterprise leaders often treat premium AI access as transformation by itself. Employees are excited to receive the fastest model, but the organization has not taught fit-for-purpose model selection, cost awareness, session shutdown habits, or value measurement. Usage scales before operating discipline does, and finance discovers the burn after it has already become normal behavior.

## Vehicle Analogy

Advanced frontier AI models are Formula 1 cars. Everyday assistants are practical sedans. Tokens, context, and lingering sessions are fuel, tire burn, and engine run time. Enablement is driver education. Governance is fleet management. Cost controls are fuel-card limits. Monitoring is telemetry and trip logs. Business value is arriving somewhere useful, not simply driving the fastest car.

## Candidate Four-Panel Flow

1. Leadership launches premium model access as a campus event. One employee receives a Formula 1 key with unrealistic excitement while a deadpan side watcher points out the commute is only two blocks.
2. The usage plan is simpler and more absurd: one race car, one excited driver promising a faster commute, one key holder thinking about a supercharged grocery run, and one sedan user disappointed that Standard Assistant does not feel enterprise.
3. One car is parked for the night with its rear end lifted by wedge machinery. The rear tires are not touching the ground but keep spinning while the driver walks away, IT questions the burn, and a business-suit manager calls it value.
4. Executives stare at a half-billion-dollar monthly AI fuel invoice. One person covers her eyes while an IT person at the door reports that users asked for more fuel credits.

## Image-Generation Notes

Use the established reference style: square 2x2 editorial page, black-and-white pen-and-ink line work, simple stick-figure workplace characters, slightly more rendered grayscale vehicles, numbered panel badges, sparse speech bubbles, and a bottom kicker. Keep all text large and readable. The car should not become a glossy supercar ad; the visual joke is operating-model mismatch.

Panel 3 is the iconic image. It must show the car as technically parked while still consuming resources: spinning rear tires, dyno rollers, token burn gauge, and active overnight monitor. This should make the user belief ("I parked it") conflict visibly with the operating reality ("it is still burning").

V2 revision note: Panel 1 consolidates the executive's rollout line and "strategic one" line into one executive-owned bubble to improve speaker alignment. Panel 3 explicitly places each speech bubble above or beside its speaker so the departing employee, Ops person, and manager each own their line.

V3 revision note: The title moves to "The Strategic Two-Block Commute" for sharper sarcasm. Panel 1 emphasizes the contrast between unrealistic key-recipient excitement and the side watcher's dry "two blocks" objection. Panel 2 is simplified to one visible race car. Panel 3 makes the parked-but-burning metaphor physical by lifting the rear tires off the ground on a wedge. Panel 4 removes explanatory dialogue and lands on the dry fuel-credit request.
