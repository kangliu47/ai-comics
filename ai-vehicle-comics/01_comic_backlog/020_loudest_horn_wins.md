# Comic 020: The Loudest Horn Wins

Fictional industry scenario. People, organizations, workflows, and figures are illustrative.

## Status

Priority concept for the product orchestration arc

## Core Message

Without product orchestration, AI prioritization goes to the loudest request, shiniest demo, or most senior sponsor instead of the work with the highest enterprise leverage.

## Core Critique

Enterprise AI portfolios often reward noise. Flashy prototypes and urgent executive requests move first while boring foundations wait: data quality, reusable pipelines, governed patterns, semantic models, intake triage, evaluation, and decision-support products.

## Vehicle Analogy

AI requests are vehicles honking at an intersection. The AI Product Lead installs traffic control: value scoring, dependency sequencing, capacity planning, and routing based on enterprise leverage.

## Audience

Executives, portfolio owners, AI councils, product leads, platform teams, and teams frustrated by prioritization theater.

## Candidate Four-Panel Flow

1. An enterprise AI intersection fills with vehicles labeled "Executive Demo," "Predictive Dashboard," "Autonomous Agent," and "Urgent Bot."
2. Everyone honks for right of way. The loudest vehicle has a giant horn labeled "Senior Sponsor."
3. Quiet but important vehicles wait at the light: "Data Quality," "Reusable Pipeline," "Semantic Model," and "Evaluation Pattern."
4. The Product Lead replaces the broken light with a dispatch board labeled "Value / Dependency / Capacity / Reuse."

## Dialogue And Labels

- Panel 1: Sign: "AI Prioritization Intersection."
- Panel 2: Team: "We need this first."
- Panel 3: Quiet vehicle label: "Shared semantic model."
- Panel 4: Product Lead: "Volume is not a prioritization criterion."

## Punchline Options

- Innovation by horn volume was not in the strategy deck.
- The loudest horn got the green light. The enterprise got traffic.
- Honking is not portfolio management.

## Image-Generation Notes

Make the intersection instantly readable. Use a few honking vehicles and two or three quiet foundational vehicles waiting patiently. The Product Lead's board should be simple and legible. Avoid making any requester look villainous; the critique is the missing prioritization system.

## Open Questions

- Should "Senior Sponsor" be on the horn or on a passenger waving from the vehicle?
- Should the quiet foundational vehicle be "Data Quality" or "Semantic Model" for faster recognition?
