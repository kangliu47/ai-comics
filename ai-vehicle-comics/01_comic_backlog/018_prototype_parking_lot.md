# Comic 018: The Prototype Parking Lot

Fictional industry scenario. People, organizations, workflows, and figures are illustrative.

## Status

Priority concept for the product orchestration arc

## Core Message

AI lowers the cost of experimentation, but without product orchestration the enterprise scales a parking lot of disconnected prototypes instead of a fleet of reusable capabilities.

## Core Critique

Enterprises mistake "it worked in the demo" for transformation. Pilots, bots, copilots, automations, and dashboards accumulate without registration, integration, ownership, maintenance, support, or reuse.

## Vehicle Analogy

AI prototypes are half-finished vehicles parked around the enterprise. The AI Product Lead creates the productization logic: retire, reuse, scale, combine, or convert into a maintained fleet capability.

## Audience

AI transformation leaders, Data & Analytics product leads, Data Science teams, enterprise architecture, enablement teams, and executives looking for proof of enterprise AI progress.

## Candidate Four-Panel Flow

1. Leadership asks, "Where is the AI transformation?" while standing at the edge of a corporate parking lot.
2. The camera widens to show many labeled vehicles: "HR Bot Pilot," "Finance Forecast Demo," "Legal Copilot Trial," "Ops Dashboard v3," and "Compliance Agent POC."
3. A worker points proudly at signs reading "Worked in demo," while every vehicle has a different charger, key, owner, and missing wheel.
4. The Product Lead arrives with a fleet board labeled "Retire / Reuse / Scale / Combine / Convert" and starts sorting the lot.

## Dialogue And Labels

- Panel 1: Leader: "Where is the enterprise AI transformation?"
- Panel 2: Lot sign: "AI Prototype Parking Lot."
- Panel 3: Team: "They all worked in the demo."
- Panel 4: Product Lead board: "Retire / Reuse / Scale / Combine / Convert."

## Punchline Options

- We didn't build an AI fleet. We built an AI parking problem.
- "Worked in demo" is not a fleet strategy.
- The parking lot was full. The capability roadmap was empty.

## Image-Generation Notes

Make the parking lot visually full but not cluttered. Use five or six vehicles maximum, with short labels. The Product Lead should be calm and product-minded, holding a dispatch board or lifecycle matrix. The final panel should show sorting logic, not heroics.

## Open Questions

- Should the last panel show a few prototypes being towed away, or keep the action at the decision-board level?
- Is "Retire / Reuse / Scale / Combine / Convert" readable enough for a panel, or should it become "Keep / Merge / Scale / Retire"?
