# Comic 006: Bad Map, Bigger Engine

Fictional industry scenario. People, organizations, workflows, and figures are illustrative.

## Status

Revised concept

## Core Message

Better models do not solve enterprise AI problems when foundational data is missing, stale, fragmented, inaccessible, or untrusted. The autopilot can be powerful and still navigate from a bad map.

## Core Critique

Enterprises often chase stronger models while ignoring whether the model has trusted information about customers, policies, products, processes, ownership, and current operating reality.

## Vehicle Analogy

Models are engines. Foundational enterprise data is the map, navigation layer, destination database, and road-sign information that guides autopilot. A stronger engine does not fix stale maps, missing destinations, or contradictory signs.

## Candidate Four-Panel Flow

1. Executives admire a shiny engine on a stand labeled "V8 Reasoning Engine."
2. Mechanics bolt the engine into an autopilot car while a product owner asks: "Did we update the map?"
3. The navigation screen shows contradictory routes and missing destinations. Map labels include "Stale Knowledge," "Missing Metadata," "Access Denied," and "Unknown Owner."
4. The car confidently drives toward a sign reading "Old Policy Road" while the real destination sits elsewhere. Leadership says, "Can we upgrade to a V12?"

## Dialogue And Labels

- Panel 1: Executive: "This engine is twice as powerful."
- Panel 2: Product owner: "Did we update the map?"
- Panel 3: Dashboard: "Navigation uncertain." Map labels: "Stale," "Missing," "Access Denied."
- Panel 4: Executive: "Can we upgrade to a V12?"

## Punchline Options

- We upgraded the engine. The map still thinks accounting moved in 2019.
- A stronger engine does not fix a bad map.
- Autopilot is only as current as the map.

## Image-Generation Notes

Keep the engine simple but visually distinct. The navigation screen should be instantly readable as the failure point. Use only three or four map labels; too many data-quality labels will clutter the strip. The final panel should show calm executive confidence while the vehicle follows obviously stale or wrong navigation.

## Open Questions

- Should this strip explicitly show "trusted source missing," or is "bad map" enough?
- Could the final line become sharper by making the executive request another benchmark instead of another engine?
