# Comic 004: The Entire AI Highway Department

Fictional industry scenario. People, organizations, workflows, and figures are illustrative.

## Status

Developed concept

## Core Message

Enterprise AI ambition often outruns the operating capacity required to make it safe, scalable, and real.

## Core Critique

The organization presents AI transformation as a mature multi-lane transportation system, but operationally assigns one engineer to perform several incompatible enterprise roles at once: platform owner, governance support, infrastructure evaluator, and first production app builder. The failure is structural fragility, not merely personal workload.

## Vehicle Analogy

The AI strategy is a highway master plan. Platforms are the vehicle fleet and maintenance depot. Governance is traffic regulation and road safety control. Infrastructure and proof-of-technology work are bridge and road engineering reviews. The flagship AI application is the first AI vehicle being built and test-driven. Scaling is the expressway on-ramp opening.

## Audience

Enterprise AI leaders, platform teams, governance and security partners, architecture review boards, and product teams who recognize the gap between executive AI roadmaps and the staffing model needed to operate them.

## Candidate Four-Panel Flow

1. A leader presents a polished boardroom slide showing a complete four-lane enterprise AI highway.
2. Behind the slide, those four lanes converge into one strained engineer trying to run the whole highway department.
3. The engineer drives the half-built first AI vehicle while fielding platform, governance, architecture, and demo interruptions.
4. The expressway on-ramp is closed while leaders sincerely ask why the organization is not scaling faster.

## Preferred Punchline

The AI roadmap had four lanes. The staffing plan had one person.

## Image-Generation Notes

Use the "First-Principles Deadpan" direction: monochrome black-and-white line art, simple stick-figure people, and more detailed systems props, vehicles, signs, barricades, blueprints, and dashboard elements. Keep the joke dry and exact. The engineer should look strained but competent, not tragic or heroic.

Panel 2 is the iconic image. Make the four lanes visibly converge into one person performing four enterprise functions. Panel 3 should stay legible: use three or four crisp interruptions, not a wall of notifications. Panel 4 should show a predictable operating-model consequence, with leadership surprised by the throughput problem it created.

Avoid slapstick martyrdom, angry caricature, dense labels, glossy sci-fi styling, and any implication that the engineer failed personally. The absurdity belongs to the operating model.

## What Breaks Under This Model

- Platform decisions stall because ownership is bottlenecked.
- Governance coverage becomes reactive rather than embedded.
- Infrastructure evaluation is delayed by constant interruption.
- Flagship application delivery slows because the same person also supports the ecosystem.
- Institutional knowledge becomes dangerously concentrated in one role.
- Execution starts depending on individual heroics rather than scalable operating design.

## Open Questions

- In Panel 3, should the First AI App vehicle be half-built to emphasize "build while driving," or visibly overloaded to read more cleanly at a glance?
- Should Panel 2 show all four platform fleet labels, or reduce to two labels to preserve legibility?
