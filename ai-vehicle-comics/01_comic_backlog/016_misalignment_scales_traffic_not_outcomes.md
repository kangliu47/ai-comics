# Comic 016: Misalignment Scales Traffic

Fictional industry scenario. People, organizations, workflows, and figures are illustrative.

## Status

Selected visual draft v2 created.

## Core Message

AI use cases scale only when the right driver has the right vehicle, permission to use the route, and enough road capacity to keep moving.

## Core Critique

Enterprises often try to scale AI by launching more pilots while leaving the operating model misaligned. Skilled teams are blocked from business routes, approved use cases depend on the same people to build the lane while driving, and sponsored efforts stall because the assigned drivers lack the needed AI/data/engineering capability. The result is more AI project traffic, not more enterprise outcomes.

## Vehicle Analogy

Capability is the skilled AI/data/engineering driver who can actually operate the vehicle. Access is the keys, route pass, dispatch pairing, decision rights, and open gate that allow the right driver to take the right vehicle onto the right business route. Capacity is the lane space, bridges, support crew, merge room, and delivery bandwidth needed for the pilot to become a highway.

This comic deliberately separates operating-model access from physical integrations: access means authorization and correct pairing; integrations remain the roads, ramps, and bridges the vehicle uses after it is allowed onto the route.

## Audience

Enterprise AI leaders, AI enablement teams, transformation offices, product and platform leaders, data/engineering leaders, and business executives trying to understand why many AI pilots do not become scaled outcomes.

## Pre-Production Design Check

- Core message: AI use cases scale when capability, access, and capacity are aligned around the right use cases.
- Causal chain: enterprises launch pilots with one pillar missing; each pilot creates local activity but cannot reach scaled outcomes; multiplying those pilots creates enterprise traffic.
- Visual contrast: individual pilots with one missing operating-model pillar versus a clean highway sign for enterprise outcomes.
- Panel logic: Panel 1 shows a capable driver blocked by missing access, Panel 2 shows approved movement constrained by missing capacity, Panel 3 shows an open route stalled by missing capability, and Panel 4 shows these local failures accumulating into system traffic.
- Final consequence: the highway jam is earned because each prior panel adds a different stuck vehicle to the enterprise road network.
- Punchline: "Misalignment scales traffic, not outcomes" is specific enough to repeat in an operating-model conversation.
- Clutter risk: keep each panel to one missing-pillar visual, one caption, one short dialogue pair, and no dense status dashboard unless a small check/cross box improves clarity.
- Overlap watch: distinct from road/integration comics because the primary failure is operating-model alignment, not missing APIs or roads.

## Candidate Four-Panel Flow

1. Capability and capacity without access: a skilled AI engineer stands beside a practical vehicle labeled "AI Use Case." The road ahead has enough capacity and points to "Scale," but a locked gate labeled "Access Required" blocks the route. The driver and vehicle are not officially paired.
2. Capability and access without capacity: the approved engineer drives the AI use-case vehicle with an "Access Pass" along a tiny "Pilot Lane" that tries to merge into the "Scale" road. The same engineer is also in a road-crew vest trying to widen the lane.
3. Capacity and access without capability: a wide open road labeled "Scale" has plenty of lanes and a valid route pass, but the assigned driver stalls the AI vehicle while holding an "Intro to AI" manual.
4. Enterprise scale: a wide highway-system view shows many AI vehicles stuck in different operating-model failure modes while a distant sign points to "Enterprise Outcomes."

## Image-Generation Notes

Use the established reference style: square 2x2 editorial comic page, minimalist black-and-white pen-and-ink line work, thick hand-drawn panel borders, black numbered panel badges, simple stick-figure workplace characters, slightly more rendered grayscale AI vehicles, sparse speech bubbles, readable signage, and a centered bottom kicker. Add a small artist signature at bottom-right: "KKLL".

Panel 1 should make the missing access visually obvious through the locked gate, badge reader, or dispatch booth. Panel 2 should make the missing capacity absurd by showing the engineer as both driver and road crew. Panel 3 should make the missing capability obvious without mocking the person: the critique is assignment mismatch, not personal stupidity. Panel 4 should be an overhead or wide highway-system view with several different stuck AI projects, but it must stay legible and avoid a dense infographic.

Use only the labels essential to the joke. Favor three or four stuck vehicles in Panel 4 over a crowded swarm. The optional three-pillar status boxes can be omitted unless the image needs more clarity; the captions already name the missing pillar.
