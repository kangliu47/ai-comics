# Comic 022: The AI Drive-Through Menu

Fictional industry scenario. People, organizations, workflows, and figures are illustrative.

## Status

Backlog concept for the product orchestration arc

## Core Message

Lowering the cost of requesting AI work does not create a product strategy unless demand is triaged, patterned, scored, and sequenced.

## Core Critique

Business teams line up to order tools, bots, dashboards, agents, and automations as if AI delivery were fast food. The loudest orders get made first, the kitchen drowns in custom requests, and nobody checks whether the meal solves an enterprise problem.

## Vehicle Analogy

The enterprise has an AI drive-through. The AI Product Lead turns random orders into a product menu with standard patterns, intake triage, value scoring, sequencing, and escalation paths for strategic opportunities.

## Audience

Analytics intake teams, business stakeholders, Data Science and Analytics Engineering leaders, AI enablement teams, and product leads designing request models.

## Candidate Four-Panel Flow

1. Business teams line up at a drive-through sign labeled "AI Orders."
2. Customers shout custom orders: "One agent with extra autonomy," "A dashboard, but predictive," and "Make my spreadsheet a copilot."
3. The kitchen is overwhelmed; Data Science is wearing a headset and taking support tickets while dashboards spill out of bags.
4. The Product Lead replaces the order chaos with a structured product menu: "Standard patterns / Triage / Value score / Strategic escalation."

## Dialogue And Labels

- Panel 1: Menu board: "Bots / Dashboards / Agents / Automations."
- Panel 2: Customer: "Can I get that with extra autonomy?"
- Panel 3: Kitchen ticket: "Undefined business problem."
- Panel 4: Product Lead: "First we decide what is actually on the menu."

## Punchline Options

- Lowering the cost of ordering did not create a nutrition strategy.
- The AI kitchen was fast. The menu was imaginary.
- Extra autonomy is not a side order.

## Image-Generation Notes

Keep the drive-through recognizable: menu board, speaker, service window, tickets. Avoid too much food detail; the joke is request intake and product menu discipline. The final board should be cleaner than the chaotic order tickets.

## Open Questions

- Is "nutrition strategy" too food-specific for a vehicle series, or does the drive-through framing justify it?
- Should Data Science be in the kitchen, or should Analytics Engineering be shown as the overwhelmed kitchen crew?
